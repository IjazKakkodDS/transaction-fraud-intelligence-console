PLAYBOOK: False Positive Identification

PURPOSE
This playbook guides analysts in identifying cases that the fraud system has flagged
in error. Minimising false positives is critical to customer experience and operational
efficiency.

COMMON FALSE POSITIVE PATTERNS

1. High-value legitimate purchase
   - Customer bought a large-ticket item (electronics, travel, jewellery).
   - Amount is above the threshold but the merchant is known and the user has prior
     high-value purchases.
   - Risk score is elevated by amount alone; model confidence is moderate.

2. Legitimate late-night transaction
   - Night transaction from a shift worker, frequent traveller, or international user.
   - User's transaction history shows consistent late-night activity.
   - No other anomalous signals present.

3. First-time merchant, legitimate use
   - User is shopping at a new retailer — merchant is new to them but is a known,
     reputable business.
   - No geographic mismatch, no velocity pattern, no suspicious IP.

4. Travel scenario
   - User is abroad; transactions appear cross-border or from an unusual location.
   - Amount and merchant type are consistent with travel spending.

INVESTIGATION STEPS
1. Review user's 30-day transaction history for behavioural baselines.
2. Check merchant reputation and whether other users transact there without fraud.
3. Confirm there are no co-occurring high-risk signals (velocity, suspicious IP, ATO indicators).
4. Contact the cardholder if the case remains ambiguous after tool-based review.

RECOMMENDATION CRITERIA
- FALSE_POSITIVE if: single signal fired, user history is consistent, no co-occurring indicators.
- ESCALATE if: context is insufficient to make a confident determination.

POLICY REFERENCES
- Fraud Policy FP-009: False positive rate must be reviewed monthly; target < 5% of REVIEW cases.
- SOP-FP-01: Before marking FALSE_POSITIVE, at least one user-history data point must be verified.
